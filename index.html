Conference papers

Morphology-Aware Nested Character Embeddings for Word Representation

IEEE ICCCMLA 2025 · Published in IEEE Xplore Gruhesh Sri Sai Karthik Kurra, Chandanasree Moparthi, Prathipati Manish Chowdary, Pavan Kumar Pagadala

A character-level Transformer builds every word from its characters. Related forms share part of their representation, and unseen words still get a vector. In a seeded re-implementation it beats word embeddings by 14.9 F1 on German NER (63.0 vs 48.2) and is on par on English topic classification.

Paper · Code · Results

Dual-Stream Artifact Detection with Iterative Evidence Refinement for Frame-Level Deepfake Recognition

IEEE NEPCON 2026 · Accepted, presentation December 2026 Gruhesh Sri Sai Karthik Kurra, Pavan Kumar Pagadala, Malathy Batumalay, Veda Boddapati

The detector is a ViT-Small model with an edge stream for blending boundaries and a frequency stream for generator fingerprints, fused by iterative cross-attention. An audit found that most WildDeepfake test frames came from training videos, so the main result is 96.3% AUC on a video-disjoint test subset (87.1% on Celeb-DF).

Code · Dataset

Prism Tuning: Repulsion-Trained Seed Embeddings in a Frozen Transformer for Non-Redundant Generation

IEEE NEPCON 2026 · Accepted, presentation December 2026 Gruhesh Sri Sai Karthik Kurra, Pavan Kumar Pagadala, Malathy Batumalay, Sushma Reddy Koduru

Only K seed vectors are trained. They are added to the input embeddings of a frozen transformer and pushed apart by a repulsion loss, so one prompt gives K different answers in a single batched pass with no filtering. The paper gives the method and formulation only. In October 2026 I tested a redesigned version in pre-registered GPU experiments on Qwen2.5. It did not significantly beat tuned min-p sampling, and the main suspected cause is the training data.

BIE: Bit-Index Encoding of Sparse Neural Network Weights

Zenodo preprint · Version 2, October 2026 Gruhesh Sri Sai Karthik Kurra

BIE is a storage format for pruned weights. The gaps between non-zeros are Golomb–Rice coded, close to their entropy limit, and the values are 8-bit codes stored as bitplanes. At 95% sparsity it is 44.5× smaller than float32 at 0.6% error, against 25.7× for CSR with the same values.
