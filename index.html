publications

  1. Morphology-Aware Nested Character Embeddings for Word Representation

    AcceptedIEEE ICCCMLA 2025 · 2025

    Abstract: Morphology-Aware Nested Character Embeddings for Word Representation

    MANCE represents each word as an ordered sequence of shared character embeddings, allowing related word forms to share information without explicit morphological supervision. The paper develops separate RNN, CNN, and hierarchical Transformer integrations and evaluates them on text classification, sentiment analysis, and named entity recognition. Reported results include 93.5% accuracy on AG News, 99.0% on DBpedia, and 81.3% F1 on German named entity recognition. In a separate controlled sentiment experiment with 50 samples, MANCE achieves 91.67% accuracy versus 33.33% for Word2Vec, with training times of 0.51 and 1.11 seconds respectively. These small-experiment gains are distinct from the broader benchmark results.

  2. BIE: Bit-Index Encoding for Efficient Neural Network Weight Compression

    AcceptedZenodo · September 2025

    Abstract: BIE: Bit-Index Encoding for Efficient Neural Network Weight Compression

    Bit-Index Encoding (BIE) compresses sparse neural network weight matrices by recording the positions of nonzero bits. Three variants address different trade-offs: binary encoding for sparse binary weights, bitplane encoding for adjustable reconstruction precision, and blocked encoding for cache locality and parallel processing. The implementation includes Numba-compiled sparse matrix multiplication kernels that operate on compressed representations. Matrix benchmarks cover dimensions from 256 × 256 to 2048 × 1024 and sparsity levels up to 95%. Binary encoding reaches 40× compression at 95% sparsity; separately, bitplane variants achieve reconstruction mean squared error below 10⁻⁶. These results concern matrix compression and reconstruction, rather than downstream model accuracy.

  3. Hybrid RAG-Enhanced Deepfake Detection: Combining Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Visual Inconsistency Analysis

    AcceptedZenodo · August 2024

    Abstract: Hybrid RAG-Enhanced Deepfake Detection: Combining Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Visual Inconsistency Analysis

    This paper proposes a local deepfake detection framework that combines retrieval-augmented generation with visual inconsistency analysis. A retrieval pipeline uses Vision Transformer embeddings and FAISS to compare an input with known authentic and manipulated samples, then supplies retrieved evidence to a local language model. A complementary visual pipeline uses object detection and segmentation to examine anatomical irregularities, facial symmetry, boundaries, and lighting. Confidence-weighted fusion combines the two sources of evidence, while uncertainty estimates identify ambiguous cases for human review. The paper specifies the architecture and evaluation protocol for privacy-preserving analysis. Its performance figures are projections; implementation and full empirical validation remain future work.

under review & working papers

  1. Prism Tuning: Repulsion-Trained Seed Embeddings in a Frozen Transformer for Non-Redundant Generation

    Under ReviewIEEE NEPCON 2026 · Submitted 2026

    Abstract: Prism Tuning: Repulsion-Trained Seed Embeddings in a Frozen Transformer for Non-Redundant Generation

    Prism Tuning proposes a parameter-efficient method for generating diverse responses from a frozen language model. K learnable seed vectors are added to input embeddings to condition parallel generation streams. During training, a repulsion objective acts on generated-position hidden states, providing a differentiable proxy for differences between decoded outputs. Whitening addresses representation anisotropy, while shared style parameters and separate content parameters aim to discourage diversity based only on tone. Quality and KL-divergence terms anchor the streams to the base model. At inference, the seeds require no coordination between streams, though generating K responses still incurs their generation cost. The paper presents the formulation and integration requirements; systematic empirical evaluation remains future work.

  2. Dual-Stream Artifact Detection with Iterative Evidence Refinement for Frame-Level Deepfake Recognition

    Under ReviewIEEE NEPCON 2026 · Submitted 2026

    Abstract: Dual-Stream Artifact Detection with Iterative Evidence Refinement for Frame-Level Deepfake Recognition

    This study combines a Sobel-edge boundary stream with a multi-band Fourier frequency stream for frame-level deepfake detection. An orthogonality penalty discourages redundant evidence, and an iterative cross-attention module refines and fuses the streams. The study uses a corpus of 227,504 frames from FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF v2, and WildDeepfake. A source-video audit found extensive train–test overlap in WildDeepfake, so the paper separates the contaminated evaluation from a video-disjoint subset. On that 5,680-frame subset, the model achieves 96.3% AUC and 90.5% accuracy; Celeb-DF alone yields 87.1% AUC. Results come from one training run, without controlled baseline comparisons or ablations, which limits claims about the contribution of individual components.

    Code and dataset: GitHub · Kaggle dataset.

  3. RADAR: Reasoning-Augmented Deepfake Artifact Recognition via Multi-Branch Evidence Aggregation

    Preprint2026

    Abstract: RADAR: Reasoning-Augmented Deepfake Artifact Recognition via Multi-Branch Evidence Aggregation

    RADAR proposes deepfake detection through the aggregation of complementary forensic evidence. Three specialised branches examine skin texture, boundary aliasing, and fingerprints associated with AI image generation. An R-Former module combines their outputs through iterative cross-attention, with per-artifact confidence scores and spatial saliency maps intended to explain predictions. The design specifies a Vision Transformer trained from random initialisation, alongside artifact supervision and an evaluation protocol covering detection, cross-dataset generalisation, and interpretability. A 200-image pilot analysis motivates the artifact branches. Full-system benchmark validation remains future work, and the paper’s performance figures are design targets rather than measured outcomes. The proposed scope is facial manipulation in individual images.
